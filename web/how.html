How it works

Five drawings: how the data gets collected, how a real place becomes a map a robot can stand on, what the policy is, how it learns, and what the robot actually computes every 20 ms.

01 · The data

Three ways in, one dataset

Phones collect quietly while people ski or hike, the webcam game collects while people play, and any video becomes motion. Every sample gets tied to the exact ground it happened on.

02 · The maps

Any place becomes physics

Type a place. Its trails and slopes come from OpenStreetMap, its shape from elevation data, and the two are stitched into a world a robot can stand on, with snow that grips and slides like snow.

03 · The policy

A ski brain on top of a body that already walks

NVIDIA's SONIC already moves a Unitree G1 like a person. Our policy sits on top: it feels the snow and the ground ahead, and nudges SONIC to lean, edge and carve.

04 · Training

Thousands of robots, one exam

Thousands of robots practice at once. Every checkpoint takes the same exam, and the slopes where robots still fall tell us what data to collect next.

05 · Under the hood

Spatial inference, 50 times a second

Every 20 ms the mountain is boiled down to numbers the policy can read: the ground ahead sampled in the robot's own frame, rays that find trees, and the pressure under each ski edge. Physics runs ten steps for every decision.
